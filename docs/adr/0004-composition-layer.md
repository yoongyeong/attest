# ADR 0004 — Shared composition layer with 主/次 groups

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Deciders:** Kevin Pratama
- **Relates to:** ADR 0001 (dev-side judge), ADR 0002 (non-blocking extractor), ADR 0003 (adversarial authorship), ARCHITECTURE.md, CONTEXT.md, BUSINESS.md

## Context

By 2026-10-16 the product had three surfaces, each rendering itself:

| Surface | Artifacts | Serve cost |
|---|---|---|
| One-shot reading | `pipeline.js` + `generator.js` (model-generated, verified) | model call |
| Daily fortune (流日) | `daily-fortune.js` + 50 precomputed fragments | zero |
| Annual forecast (流年) | `annual-forecast.js` + 66 precomputed fragments | zero |

Two problems surfaced:

1. **Surfaces were unconnected.** Adding a fourth would mean wiring it to each existing
   surface — N² connections, and every addition reopened prior work.
2. **Composition quality was uneven.** The annual interleaved its two fragment sets and
   read well. A naive daily+annual merge produced a visible seam: two openings back to
   back in `overall`, because both sets answer the *same* question ("what is happening")
   at different timescales.

An architectural choice was required, and it was evaluated against direct
surface-to-surface wiring and a separate pair-based composer (compatibility 合婚).

## Decision

**A shared composition layer.** Surfaces register fragment *sets*; the layer composes
them. Four commitments:

### 1. Composition groups (主/次)

Sets declare a `group` and a `role` (`primary` | `secondary`).

- **Same group** → the sets stand in a primary/secondary relation and **compose** within
  a section. `annual-k1` (what the year is about) + `annual-k2` (how it lands) compose.
- **Different groups** → both are primaries. They do **not** merge; the lower-priority
  one is **held back** and exposed separately for the surface to render in its own block.

This mirrors the BaZi principle **主次** (primary/secondary): secondary factors *modify*
the primary, they never contradict it. Two primaries have no such relationship, so
merging them manufactures a statement that does not exist.

### 2. Epistemic separation

Every set declares `verified-fact` or `interpretation`. Verified facts render before
interpretation and the registers never blend. This is what stops an
unfalsifiable-by-design reading from drifting into making falsifiable claims — and it
supports the positioning in BUSINESS.md ("concrete accuracy + unfalsifiable
interpretation, side by side").

Verified facts are generated by **code**, never by a model.

### 3. Provenance

The composer records which set, group, and fragment key produced each contribution.
This is the accountability view from CONTEXT.md made implementable: hand-wired text has
no traceable origin; composed text does.

### 4. Collision log

When contributions meet in a section the composer **records** the collision — who led,
who composed, who was held back, and why. Genuine tension is *named*, not silently
smoothed, the same way a clash is recorded in a chart rather than cancelled.

## Rationale

- **Testability.** One composer tested once, versus N² hand-written seams each needing
  its own test and invalidating on every new surface.
- **Liability surface.** Every hand-written seam is a sentence no checker validates.
  Fewer hand-written transitions means fewer unchecked claims — the same argument as
  ADR 0002 and the fragment spec.
- **Additivity.** A fourth single-chart surface costs fragments plus a `register()` call.
- **Domain-derived, not invented.** The group rule is 主次, not a preference. That also
  makes it explainable to a practitioner.
- **Validation on a known-good case.** The layer was required to reproduce the annual's
  existing bespoke composition exactly before being trusted. It did: **20/20 sections
  byte-identical** across 4 charts, after the annual's sets were migrated to the layer.

## Consequences

- `annual-forecast.js` was refactored; K1 and K2 are now registered through the layer
  (`annual-sets.js`). Verified byte-identical against a pre-refactor baseline
  (`baseline-annual.json`).
- Adding a surface requires: fragments, a `register()` call, and a declared group/role.
  No wiring to other surfaces.
- A **pair-based** surface (compatibility) is *not* covered by this decision: it keys to
  two charts, a different key shape, and will need its own composition path.
- The **heldBack** mechanism means a cross-timescale view shows one set's sections and
  holds the other's. The presentation decision (stacked, labelled) belongs to the entry
  point, not the composer.
- **Composition cannot resolve semantic conflict.** Whether two fragments contradict is
  a semantic property code cannot judge. The mitigation is upstream: fragments written
  in a `secondary` role are prompted as *modifiers* by construction (as annual-k2 was).

## Verification

- Annual reproduced exactly through the layer: 20/20 sections identical.
- Group behaviour confirmed: same-group composes; cross-group holds back.
- Collision log emits the reason for every meeting.
- `profile.js` (unified entry point) renders stacked with `[VERIFIED]` / `[READING]`
  registers distinct.

## Not decided here

- Presentation/ordering policy for stacked views (currently: facts, day, year, decade).
- Whether a synthesis fragment set should be authored to say something genuinely
  cross-timescale ("your clash year is showing up this week").
- The pair-based composition path for compatibility.
- Cache/versioning policy as fragment sets evolve.
